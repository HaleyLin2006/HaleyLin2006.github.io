My high school, supervised by Dr. Jui-Chung (Allen), Li and Dr. Pei-Yu, Tsai (we call her Prof. Tsai), was definitely an odd one. Our curriculum is centered around "problem-based learning" in which we are encouraged (forced, to an extent) to read scholarly article and build *our own* academic taste. 

I wanted to be a theoretical physicist at the time but Allen predicted that "you would never be a good physicist" when we first met. Luckily, he was a great quantitative sociologist who is somehow more of a neo-classical liberal so he convinces both me and Prof. Tsai to take economics seriously. Later, Prof. Tsai taught us basically equivalent of intermediate micro in our math class and that's how I got into economics.

I was exposed to the Economics classics early on. I read Morgenstein and von Neumann's game theory book for a math class. How they build utility from axioms of revealed preference still fascinate me. I was taking Real Analysis at the time so the math was no stranger to me. But that was the first time I witness firsthand how  math can be used to model human behaviour and that excites me. I read Coase's social cost poorly but I still can't stop thinking about his Nature of Firm paper. And Sen is still a mystery to me.

Looking back, to a certain degree, it is definitely somewhat inhumanitarian to throw high school student papers like Acemoglu, Johnson, and Robinson (2001) without the proper tool. But I remember I was happy to be immersed in all these smart ideas. **Moreover,  I was fearless. And I shall not let anyone take that away.** It is definitely through this process of trial-and-error that I built my "critical thinking" skills. It took me around three years to finally get when am I bullshitting and when do I truly understand the essence of the paper and can conduct an intellectual conversation about it. It is brutal both physically and mentally (I cried ~3 times with Prof. Tsai and froze during Q&A in presentation with Allen countless times), but it is the best education you can ever hope.

One of our high school folks just got into YC. Things move fast. So I guess it is good to have this down.
